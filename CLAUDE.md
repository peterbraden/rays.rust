# CLAUDE.md - Rays.rust Project Guide

## Project Overview
Rays.rust is a raytracer written in Rust with the following features:
- Whitted tracing and path tracing with Monte-Carlo global illumination
- Material models: Lambertian, Specular, Dielectric, Plastic, Leaf (translucent, `src/material/leaf.rs`), procedural Rock (layered by elevation and bed, festoon cross-bedding shared with terrain relief (`rock::cross_beds`), honeycomb pits, desert varnish, flutes; `fractal` turbulent bump mapping down to the pixel's footprint, and `crackle` fractures, for close-up detail) and Soil (`src/material/soil.rs`: mottled topsoil, stones, litter and moss; loam, clay, sand, desert, forest_floor); snow, glacier ice and snow cover (`src/material/snow.rs`, docs/snow-and-ice.md: colours from ice's measured absorption and the size of grains or bubbles, by asymptotic radiative transfer; Kokhanovsky and Bréon's snow reflection with forward scattering; soot and dust; grain glints; glacier ice as a Fresnel surface over bubble-diffused light; icebergs (`"iceberg"`: tabular, blocky, dome, pinnacle, wedge, drydock, floating by buoyancy) and pack ice (`"ice_floes"`, or `"style": "brash"`: lumps of glacier ice) in `src/procedural/iceberg.rs`, with submerged rams seen through `"transparent": true` water (the ocean traces refracted rays to what is submerged; `Geometry::transmits` lets shadow rays up through the surface), walls broken in conchoidal scoops, blue where freshly broken and white where weathered, under a CIE overcast sky (`"background": {"type": "overcast"}`), `demo/scenes/icebergs.json`); bump mapping via an object's "normal" (src/material/bump.rs, docs/bump-mapping.md); `surface_layers` (`src/material/surface_layers.rs`, docs/surface-layers.md): Terragen-style ground, materials stacked bottom up, each masked by a function graph, which on a landscape (its `"material"`) can read the simulation's fields (`graph::Field`, `Medium::fields`, `src/terrain/fields.rs`: sediment, ice, flow, wetness, convexity, snowline...; `demo/scenes/alpine-layers.json`)
- Procedural materials (docs/procedural-materials.md): POV-Ray-style patterns coloured by `color_map`s (`textures::PatternTexture`, entries may be textures: pigment maps), from function graphs with sources beyond Perlin and Worley (`src/patterns.rs`: sparse Gabor and phasor noise, anisotropic and filtered by their band; gradient, rings, spherical, checker, brick and `wave` shapes box filtered from their integrals; cells' `f2`/`id`, metrics, jitter; `bombing`, leaves or discs strewn over the ground; a `transform` node), read filtered to the pixel's footprint (`graph::Context::at_hit_filtered`) and in the object's own space less its scaling (`Geometry::pattern_frame`, `Context::at_hit_in_object`); bumps from the same graphs (`"normal": {"graph"}`, metres) carried by materials (`MaterialModel::shading_normal`, `bump::BumpedMaterial`), roughness maps on plastic and conductors, inline material definitions; an `occlusion` node (short rays: dirt in crevices, wear on edges; graph contexts carry the scene, and keep scene directions in object frames, `Context::in_scene`); and a library of presets (`{"type": "library", "name", "scale", "seed"}`, `src/material/library.json` built in, and a scene's own from a top-level `"library"` (files read at run time through `assets`, or inline; `library::load`, carried to the parser in the materials map under `library::SCENE_PRESETS`; their contents in the render key): marbles, granites, quartz, limestone, basalt, slate, concrete, terrazzo, tiles, plaster, mud, asphalt, woods, metals, leather, cork, paint, linen, terracotta, brick; variables are parameters set by fields of their names; overlays over any material, `"over"`: dust, grime, rain_streaks, moss, lichen, map_lichen, wet, mud_splash, worn_edges; for natural landscapes, multi-scale and filtered from centimetres to hundreds of metres: granite_outcrop, limestone_cliff, gneiss, schist, scree, river_gravel, dune_sand, beach_sand, mudflat, oak/pine/birch/beech bark), fitted to CC0 scans by `scripts/fit-material.py` (renders the preset as the scan was taken, top-down, lit by a sun so one sample gives the albedo; colours solved by least squares through optimal transport (`ColourSolve`), and scored by every part of a colour, structure and palette loss (`PaletteLoss`: k-means colours' shares, patch sizes, adjacency and spectra) over the scan's own variation between its tiles (`FloorLoss`, 1 ≈ the scan against itself); searches by CMA-ES, with `--free` every number in the preset's graphs; each preset's scans, what to search and how the fit went kept in its entry's `references`, which the renderer ignores (it reads only a preset's `material`, `variables`, `seed_direction`, `overlay` and `scale`); scans fetched by `scripts/fetch-material-reference.py`; fits applied after review by `scripts/apply-fit.py`, a fitted size kept as the preset's own `scale`; presets modelled on scans, fitted and applied: pebble_bed, angular_rubble, mossy_slab, ochre_crust, lichened_ironstone, bedded_sandstone, trail_dirt, laterite, foliated_rock, shingle, veined_basalt, weathered_grey_rock, weathered_pink_granite, caliche_ground, stony_bank, leaf_litter_gravel (its leaves by a `bombing` node: texture bombing, sprites scattered and overlapping) (colours fitted as groups: a lightness gain, small tint shifts and a contrast, held near the author's); docs/material-fit.png, material-fit-scans.png), shown by `scripts/material-gallery.py` (docs/material-library.jpg; natural ones as ground from eye height and hills from above, or on a trunk: docs/material-library-natural.jpg)
- Objects: Sphere, Plane, Mesh, OBJ file import; transforms and instancing of named "geometries" (emitters stretched by a transform are area lights too: `Transform::sample_from`, by the item's own sampling and the stretch of its area); POV-Ray's shapes (cylinder, cone, torus, blob, isosurface, text and more; see docs/shapes.md, parsed in src/scenefile_shapes.rs), combined by CSG (src/shapes/csg.rs); mountains (`"mountains"`: ridged-noise heightfield with snow, rock and vegetation; `"cloud"` raises hill cloud over it, `src/procedural/hill_cloud.rs`: damp air lifted past saturation caps the summits and streams downwind, joining an `overcast` deck's volume (`volume::Sum`) to be lit through its column), and a planet (`"planet"`: the ground as a sphere of the Earth's radius); clouds can cover the whole planet (`"mapping": "global"`: an equi-angular cube-sphere weather map and a planet-wide wind field), seen from orbit in `demo/scenes/orbit.json` (and the bare atmosphere at the limb and from the stratosphere: `limb-sunset.json`, `stratosphere.json`)
- Participating media: boxes or closed shapes (`"shape"`: media inside dielectrics) of constant, noise-driven, function-graph (`volume::GraphDensity`; reading a landscape's fields when after one, `ObjectFields`) or voxel-grid density (`src/volume/grid.rs`: Mitsuba `.vol`, pbrt's DDA majorant grid; delta tracking with local majorants, ratio-tracked shadows); chromatic media (`"sigma_a"`/`"sigma_s"` per channel) tracked by pbrt's spectral MIS (`src/volume/chromatic.rs`: a hero channel per path, ratios settled at the nearest hit and applied as `chromatic::weight()` where light is gathered; shadow transmittance is a `Color`); emissive media (`src/volume/emission.rs`: colour or blackbody temperature grids); Henyey-Greenstein or Mie (water droplet) phase (an HG-Draine fit; and the exact Mie series for small droplets, `"mie": {"radius", "variance"}`, `src/material/mie.rs`, for direct sunlight at a path's first scattering like the rainbow's table, `rainbow::DropletPhase`: the glory and cloudbow, `demo/scenes/glory.json`), with delta-M truncation of its forward peak wherever the camera doesn't see the medium directly (`"delta_m"`, on by default: `Volume::truncated` for segments leaving a surface, `volume::at_vertex`; the camera's first scattering keeps the whole phase function); diffraction by particles (coronae, iridescence); ice-crystal halos traced through hexagonal prisms (sun dogs, 22°/46° halos, circumzenithal arc); rainbows traced through raindrops (`"rainbow": true`: primary and secondary bows, Alexander's dark band; `{"drop_radius", "spread"}` by Airy's wave optics, `RainbowTable::airy`: supernumerary bows, and the fogbow of small droplets, `demo/scenes/fogbow.json`); optional similarity and Oz multiple-scattering approximations, and diffusion for thick decks (`"diffusion"`: at a path's first vertex in the medium, sunlight diffused through a slab as thick as the column through it, `DensityField::column_depths`, and point/area lights (lightning) glowing out through the medium by the diffusion dipole (as Jensen et al.'s BSSRDF; the surface from the density's gradient, `VolumeMaterial::inward`), along the line to each sample, `VolumeMaterial::diffused_gain`, through `material::functions::sample_lights_through`; later vertices there skip both); sun probes (`VolumeMaterial::sun_probe`, on by default, `"sun_probes"`): the next vertex's sunlight by MIS of the path's continuation and a probe drawn from the phase lobe about the sun, which removes most fireflies of sunlit forward-scattering clouds (the weight reaches the next vertex through `NEXT_SUN_WEIGHT`, keyed by the continuation ray); glitter probes likewise for the sea's glitter of the sun lighting clouds from below (`VolumeMaterial::glitter_probe`, `ocean::Sea`: directions from Cox and Munk's slopes, `NEXT_GLINT_WEIGHT` read by the ocean); equi-angular sampling of lamps' glow (`"equiangular": true` on a medium, `volume::equiangular_inscattering`, after Kulla and Fajardo: along every ray of a path a light and a point about it, weighted by MIS against the collisions' own light samples, `volume::mis_weights`; both, and every collision, choose lights from the light BVH by the phase function too, `light_bvh::Lobe`, whose distance density is a stand-in, `distance_heuristic`, as weights need only add to one; a collision finds its ray's stretch by `EQUIANGULAR_RAY`; path tracer only, the kernels refuse such a medium)
- Skysphere with Rayleigh and Mie Scattering (radiance per unit of the `"sun"`'s irradiance: with a sun the sky's `brightness` follows its `intensity`, `SceneFile::sky_follows_sun`), and aerial perspective (distant objects attenuated and hazed by the same atmosphere); `"crepuscular_rays": true` lets clouds and terrain shadow the air (sunbeams)
- The rest of the universe (`"background": {"type": "space"}`, `src/procedural/space/`, docs/space.md): procedural stars (luminosity function, black-body colours), the Milky Way's glow and dust lanes, emission, reflection, planetary and dark nebulae, and spiral and elliptical galaxies; physically bright (magnitudes against the sun's, so it takes a night exposure, `"output": {"exposure": ...}`) unless `brightness` scales it; seen through a skysphere's air by its transmittance, and skipped where the sky outshines it (`threshold`) (`demo/scenes/deep-space.json`, `night-orbit.json`)
- Moons (`"moon"`, `src/celestial/`, docs/moon.md): real spheres at their true distance (placed by `direction`, a Keplerian `orbit` or `position`; tidally locked, with libration) with a seeded cratered surface (Pike's crater shapes, saturated crater populations, basins, near-side maria, rays; relief shadows near the terminator) reflecting the sun by Hapke's model fitted to the Moon's phase curve (so physically bright), lit by earthshine and eclipsed by the planet; its reflected light lights the scene and the night sky (`Moonlight`, `Scene::moonlight`, sampled like the sun via `Scene::directional_lights`); `demo/scenes/moon.json`, `moon-crescent.json`
- Planets (docs/planets.md, `src/celestial/`): gas and ice giants (`"gas_giant"`: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune presets or seeded; oblate spheroids, `shape.rs`; cloud bands grown from the jets' shear and the clouds advected by jets, turbulence and vortices, `bands.rs`; Hapke cloud-deck photometry calibrated to geometric albedo, `giant.rs`; moons' shadows on them), rings (`rings.rs`: optical-depth profiles, Saturn's measured, seeded ones with resonance gaps; single scattering lit/unlit faces, transmission, shadows both ways, filtered τ), rocky and icy worlds as `moon` presets (`terrain.rs`: mercury, mars, callisto, ganymede, europa, io, seeded rocky/icy), and `"world"`: any of these from one seed (`celestial::world_object`); `demo/scenes/saturn.json`, `saturn-shadows.json`, `jupiter.json`, `worlds.json`
- Lightning (`"lightning"`, `src/procedural/lightning.rs`): a channel grown as a stepped leader in the storm's field (`Field`: a charge tripole, the channel's charge, images in the ground; tips advance with probability (E/E_max)^η as in the dielectric breakdown model, 16° tortuosity, branching thinning by generation, the race to the ground decides the main channel; fractal detail by midpoint displacement; branches glow by the channel beyond them), cloud-to-ground or intracloud, the in-cloud channels grown as positive leaders through a charge layer (`Field::layer`); upward connecting leaders and losing streamers, striking distance from `peak_current`; a flash in time (`Flash`: return strokes, dart leaders lighting the main channel only, continuing current; what an exposure from `time` for `shutter` catches); physically bright (lumen-seconds per metre per stroke over the `shutter`, on the sun's scale, `SceneFile::lightning_follows_sun`) in its measured line spectrum (`spectrum::lightning`, `Emission::Lightning`); an emitter sampled segment by segment through its own light BVH (`Channel::sample_from`), widened to half a pixel from the camera keeping its light per metre, casting no shadow; `demo/scenes/lightning.json`, `lightning-anvil.json`
- Directional sun light (`"sun"`), sampled directly; follows the skysphere's sun and is reddened by its atmosphere
- Procedural objects including Ocean (Tessendorf's algorithm with Phillips spectrum; the planet's surface by default, or a plane with `"planet_radius": 0`: displaced near the camera, physically lit, with sun glitter and slopes calibrated to Cox and Munk, wave height from the wind, `significant_wave_height`: Pierson-Moskowitz, or JONSWAP with a `fetch`; choppy crests by the first-order Lagrangian displacement, `choppiness`, the height field found through the inverted map, `WaveField::source`; waves break where that map's Jacobian nears folding, `ocean::Foam`: fresh foam there, torn into filaments, and residual foam riding with its water and fading over seconds behind each crest, thinning into lace, drawn into streaks in a gale, with bubbles paling the water, the whole calibrated to Monahan's coverage; scenes set `wind` to their weather, or `"wind": "weather"` takes a `"weather"` object's surface wind at the origin, `Weather::surface_wind`) and volumetric clouds: cumulus to stratus (`"nubis_clouds"`, after Horizon Zero Dawn's Nubis) and cirrus with fallstreaks (`"hogan_kew_cirrus"`, after Hogan and Kew; `"species"`: uncinus, fibratus, floccus, spissatus, `CirrusSpecies`), altocumulus as convection cells (`"benard_altocumulus"`: Rayleigh-Bénard cells, rows, undulatus waves, `castellanus` turrets), lenticular clouds over a mountain's lee waves (`"lenticular"`: stacked plates from moist layers), mammatus under an anvil (`"mammatus"`), cumulonimbus (`"cumulonimbus"`, `src/procedural/cumulonimbus.rs`: a tower shaped by parcel theory, its overshooting top w / N from CAPE, glaciating above `glaciation`, a flanking line, and an anvil carried by the wind aloft), the overcast of bad weather (`"overcast"`, `src/procedural/overcast.rs`: a nimbostratus deck kilometres thick whose underside hangs in lumps along the wind, with scud below; lit through `"diffusion": true`), fog pooled in valleys (`"valley_fog"`, `src/procedural/valley_fog.rs`: cold air over the lowest ground within `pool_size`, found by casting rays down onto the scene's own ground, filled `depth` m (a graph) or to an inversion's `top`, billowed top, haze above; `demo/scenes/valley-fog.json`), rain shafts and virga (`"precipitation"`: Marshall-Palmer extinction by rain rate, shear, evaporation; `kind` snow (Gunn-Marshall flakes, slow fall, `melting_level`) or hail (`hail_size`)), fractus (`"fractus"`, `src/procedural/fractus.rs`: torn shreds of low cloud drifting on the wind; also the overcast deck's scud under its breaks), the clouds of a storm's gust front (`"shelf_cloud"` and `"roll_cloud"`, `src/procedural/arcus.rs`: arcus, a tiered wedge under the storm's base with a ragged rising underside, and volutus, a detached tube; `demo/scenes/gust-front.json`), and cumulus grown by simulating a sunny day (`"harris_cumulus"`: stable fluids with buoyancy, condensation, latent heat and subgrid turbulence, through the ARM shallow-cumulus case's day; cached so `scripts/animate.py` can render it boiling and dissipating; detail below the grid by Kim et al.'s wavelet turbulence, `HarrisCumulus::turbulent`: noise octaves at texture coordinates the simulation carries with the flow as displacements (`CloudSimulation::texture`, two sets renewed in turn), scaled by the Smagorinsky sub-grid energy; `"hot_spot"` heats a patch of ground more, `"bubble"` seeds a saturated warm ellipsoid to start from; `"sounding": "weisman_klemp"` and `"ice": true` (mixed-phase saturation and latent heat of freezing) grow a thunderstorm to the tropopause, and `"rain": true` makes rain a field of its own (Kessler: autoconversion, accretion, evaporative cooling, terminal fall, drag; graupel below freezing; `CloudSimulation::precipitate`), `demo/scenes/cumulonimbus-simulated.json`)
- Weather (`"weather"`, `src/weather/`, docs/weather.md): a whole planet's weather on a random seed (printed; `choose_seeds` fills it in before the render is keyed) from the climate's background (ITCZ, subtropical highs, storm tracks, afternoon convection; the landscape's land and climate when there is one) and synoptic systems (`weather/systems.rs`: Norwegian-model cyclones with warm, cold and occluded fronts, anticyclones, tropical cyclones), evaluated into cube maps per family and expanded (`weather::scene::expand`) into one global volume each (`"weather_layer"`): low and mid stratiform cloud (`fields::Stratiform`; cumuliform low cloud as cauliflower heaps, `fields::Heaps`: Bouthors-Neyret trees of spheres from `procedural::cauliflower`, generated from hashes, searched with pruning, on a lattice whose features count only if their projection stays in their cell (`fields::owns`), bounded by clearance to the nearest heap), cumulus congestus and cumulonimbus towers with anvils (`fields::Convective`: clusters of columns of stacked thermals, `cauliflower::tower`, their heads rising smoothly as storms grow, tops glaciating smooth (`cauliflower::glaciated`), anvils and shields of thinner ice, `cauliflower::ICE`); clouds form as translucent wisps and fray as they die (`cauliflower::Surface::condensed`, heaps' `lifetime`), Hogan-Kew cirrus driven by a cover map (`GlobalCirrus::with_cover`), and rain, snow and virga (`fields::Rain`); `"time"` (s) animates it continuously (systems move, mature, die and are reborn in slots; noise drifts and evolves; shapes drift with `fields::Drift`; convective cells live out a life cycle); `"map"` writes a satellite view with the fronts (`demo/scenes/weather-orbit.json`, `weather-cold-front.json`, `weather-orbit-map.gif`)
- Procedural trees and bushes (Weber and Penn's parametric model, `src/procedural/tree.rs`; bushes: shrub, hazel, hawthorn, juniper; desert: creosote-bush, joshua-tree, utah-juniper), and desert plants grown as tree species with a `form` (`src/procedural/succulent.rs`: saguaro, barrel-cactus, prickly-pear, agave, ocotillo, and `mound` shrubs sagebrush and rabbitbrush; body as "bark", spines as "leaves"; in `demo/scenes/stress-test.json`), forests of instanced
  trees (`src/procedural/forest.rs`; `"lod"` sees each tree at a level of detail for its pixel size, `src/shapes/lod.rs`, docs/adaptive-meshes.md: bark simplified by vertex clustering, leaves consolidated into cards that keep their leaves' normals and faces, each hit shaded as one leaf chosen by visible area; compact `CardSet`s and `forest::Placed` instances (28 bytes), trees grown one at a time; large clusters' cards shadowed by their own leaves at the leaves' albedo (`self_lit`); clusters of 64 pieces or more crossed by up to three cards (`crossed`), a ray leaving one passing its siblings; which card a ray leaves is told by the vertex the integrator names (`src/shapes/leaving.rs`: the path tracer at each surface vertex, the kernels' host around each host vertex and its next ray, `kernels::host::HOST_VERTEX`), not by how far it starts off the card; a forest's `"cache"` keeps each distinct tree's levels and the forest's placements on disk (`src/procedural/tree_cache.rs`, `lod::Detail::encode`/`decode`; renders identical with or without it); `demo/scenes/forest-aerial.json`), and rocks (`bedrock`, `boulder`, `pebbles` objects; `rock` material); placement by rules (docs/ecosystems.md: forests, scatters and grass take a `"where"` graph read on the ground, with slope, altitude, noise, the landscape's fields and `biome`, and can stand on the `"landscape"`; `"ecosystem"`: populations placed largest first, competing for room by base and canopy shade, `procedural::ecosystem::Occupancy`, about the camera by default, `demo/scenes/alpine-meadow.json`); scatters (`"scatter"`, `SceneFile::parse_scatter`, docs/scatter.md: copies of any geometry or seeded boulders strewn over a surface or the scene's `"landscape"` by a `density` graph (reading its fields), spaced as a forest's trees, leaning with the slope; instanced transforms in a `GeometrySet`)
- Landscapes evolved through time (`"landscape"`, `src/terrain/`: uplift, rivers, glaciers, volcanoes, meanders) on a planet shaped by plate tectonics (`src/terrain/tectonics.rs`: rigid plates on Euler poles, ridges and ocean floor deepening with age, subduction arcs and trenches, continental collision and suturing, rifting, hotspots), with geology (`src/terrain/geology.rs`, docs/geology.md: a folded sedimentary cover over crystalline basement with granite plutons, `Planet::structure` per point; each `Rock` eroded at its own rate by rivers and by glaciers, `Terrain::erodibility`/`glacial_erodibility`; the belts' folds as phasor noise along the belt (`geology::fold`: an even profile of the phase, so independent of the trend's sign; leaning towards the foreland, `Trend::foreland`, with thrusts repeating the beds on their forelimbs), `geology::Trend` from the structure tensor of the belts' strength, standing as relief, uplifted on anticlines and exposing their beds as erosion cuts down, the rock carried up with the ground, `Terrain::exhumed`; thrust sheets carrying rock, folds and ground towards the foreland through the belt's fixed uplift (`Terrain::carry`, whole-cell shifts, structure from each cell's origin), rifts of tilted fault blocks, half-grabens with their strata, still slipping (`Planet::extension`/`fault_blocks`, `geology::fault_blocks`, `"near": "rift"`: basin and range), plateaus of flat strata raised without folding (`Planet::plateau`, `"near": "plateau"`: a subtropical plateau with a hard caprock) and hillslopes creeping with the rain (bare, weathering-limited slopes in deserts), coasts (`src/terrain/marine.rs`: deltas filling to sea level outwards from the mouth along the river's jet, avulsing channels switching their lobes, `Terrain::persist`; cliffs cut back by waves, `erode_coast`; longshore drift, `drift`; sea level falling 120 m with the ice ages, `Terrain::sea_level`, the sea flooding what it reaches), closed basins (lakes whose evaporation outweighs their catchment's Budyko runoff never spill: terminal salt lakes and playas, their catchment cut from the rivers below, `Terrain::basins`, `evaporation`; drawn as salt crust and brine, a `playa` field; `"cover_map"` writes the ground cover from above), wind-blown sand (`src/terrain/aeolian.rs`: where it is too dry for plants, the fine share of fresh sediment blown along the prevailing wind and dropped where it slackens, in lees and where plants take hold; a gravel lag armours the rest; `Terrain::sand`, a `sand` field; drawn as dunes across the wind, slip faces downwind, barchans where the sand is thin, `Relief::dunes`), karst (limestone lowered by solution with the rain, its rain sinking to rise at springs below it, `Terrain::dissolve`/`route`), hillslopes creeping faster on weak rock, `fluvial::diffuse_varying`; fractured anticline crests and thrust zones, `Fold::fracture`, quarried by glaciers far more than rivers, `geology::fractured`; landslides where slopes exceed their rock's critical slope (`Rock::critical_slope`, not under ice; the noise topography relaxed to it at time zero), `fluvial::landslide`; `rock` and `hardness` fields, bare rock coloured by its lithology, `"geology_map"`) glaciers sliding and eroding only where their beds are at the melting point (`GlacierParams::basal_melting`: cold thin ice frozen to its bed), carrying half of what they erode to their margins as debris, lodged as till where they melt and heaped in moraines at the margin (`glacier::carry_debris`, `Terrain::till`, a `till` field), the crust's isostatic response to erosion, deposition and ice (`src/terrain/isostasy.rs`: Airy deflection smoothed over the flexural length, relaxing with the mantle's time constant; rock carried up with it), rain made by the terrain (`src/terrain/orographic.rs`: moist air carried along the climate's prevailing wind, condensing where lifted above its condensation level, falling a fallout time downwind, rain shadows in the lee) gathered by the rivers as discharge and followed by glaciers' snow, and a climate powered by latitude (`src/terrain/climate.rs`: insolation from axial tilt, an energy balance model for temperature, Hadley/Ferrel/polar cells for winds and rain, moisture carried inland with rain shadows, Köppen-style biomes; sets glaciers' snowlines and the ground cover; `"climate_map"` writes a biome map); without `"scale"` drawn at full scale over the whole planet (`src/terrain/scenery.rs`: a whole-planet simulation with patches nested about the camera, each driven by the one around it, and a close level five times finer than the finest for a camera within 2 km of the ground (`world::close_level`, `"close_level"`; the finest's glaciers taken over, `world::take_glaciers`); below the finest cells, gullies down the slope that branch octave by octave (`Relief::gullies`); a geometry clipmap finest under the camera; ground cover from the simulations and climate; `demo/scenes/landscape.json`, `landscape-orbit.json`, `"planet_map"` writes a world map), evolved terrain cached by `"cache"`, which for a full-scale landscape is its world directory: the landscape is built by `src/world.rs` as stages (plates, climate, site, the planet's simulation, each patch), each keyed by a hash of its parameters and the keys of the stages it reads and kept under that key beside a manifest (`world-<key>.json`: stages, status, seconds, pictures), with checkpoints so a build stops and resumes, and progress as `world::Progress` events (`world::build` takes a callback; scene parsing reports through `world::report`, which prints unless a host calls `set_reporter`); a new stage of a world belongs there, with its key; ground cover uses physical snow (ageing to firn near the snowline) and glacier ice (white surface ice, blue bands, debris, crevasses where the ice steepens), and the load reports where the glaciers are (`demo/scenes/glacier.json`, a colder climate by `"warming"`)
- Render-time displacement of terrain (`"displacement"` on a `terrain`, `src/shapes/displaced.rs`, docs/displacement.md): a smooth Catmull-Rom base diced per heightmap cell to the camera's pixel size, lazily, moved along its normal by a function graph (`src/graph.rs`: fractal, cells/stones, strata, warp, terrace, slope/altitude masks, blend, expressions; ranges by interval arithmetic, octaves filtered to the vertex spacing; also usable as a colour texture, eg. a `mix`'s `amount` by slope), crack-free between levels; `vector_displacement` (`displaced::Sideways`: x, y, z and `outward` from the slope) for overhangs (`demo/scenes/overhangs.json`); faded out at the grid's edges by `displacement_falloff`; shaded where each hit was displaced from (`Geometry::undisplaced`, `Intersection::texture_point`, `graph::Context::at_hit`), so masks and rock colours line up with the relief; cells bounded by how far their slopes let the normal carry the displacement, diced `offscreen` times coarser outside the camera's view, and seen undiced by guesses (`displaced::roughly`, the sky's sampling table); ## Browser Scene Builder (prototype, `web/`)
- Build the renderer for the browser: `scripts/build-web.sh` (needs `wasm-bindgen-cli` at the version in `web/Cargo.lock`); writes `web/www/pkg`
- Serve the repository root and open `/web/`: `python3 -m http.server 8000`, then http://localhost:8000/web/ (or `cargo run --release -- serve`, below)
- One browser module (the crate in `web/`, wasm-bindgen) holds both renderers: the GPU path tracer (`Renderer`, WebGPU, for scenes that flatten) and the CPU one (`Preview`, every scene). The page uses the GPU when it can (in `web/gpu-worker.js`; the CPU's workers are `web/worker.js`) and says why when it can't. A scene is built once and rendered from any camera: a camera move keeps the built scene on the CPU (`Preview::set_camera`) and on the GPU (`Renderer::set_camera`: no flattening, buffers or kernels again), and only an edit rebuilds. The page has two tabs (`showPage`): Scene (outline, properties and scene file, with the picture small beside them) and Render (the picture alone, with the render's controls); one canvas, so the render carries on across them. `web/www/` is the GPU renderer's own test page
- The renderer is the `rays` library; the command line is its default `cli` feature. Code the library needs must build for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`: read files through `crate::assets`, use `web_time::Instant` rather than `std::time::Instant`, and no threads outside rayon
- `src/preview.rs` is the interface the page drives (build from JSON, render rows of a layer, set camera, pick)
- The realtime raycaster (`src/raycaster.rs`, `web/raycaster.js`): one unbounced ray per pixel, shown while the camera is being moved, and the ray `pick` casts; how a hit becomes a colour is a `Shading` (`headlight` so far; `?shading=` on the page), so a new view is a variant and its arm in `Raycaster::cast`. It builds nothing: displaced terrain is seen as its heightmap's cells (`displaced::roughly`), and the sky is evaluated on a lattice of every fourth pixel (`SkyLattice`); `measure_raycast` (ignored, `RAYS_SCENE=`) times it on a scene by object
- A landscape's world reaches the browser from its `cache`: `SceneFile::world_manifests` names the manifests, the page fetches them and the stage files they list and `provide`s them, and `world::Store` reads provided files where it can't write; keys must come out the same on every platform (a browser's libm differs in the last bit: quantise what trigonometry gives before keying, as patches' latitudes are). Skies' tables are kept likewise (`skysphere::table_file`, `sky-<key>.bin` in the skysphere's `cache`, by default the landscape's; `SceneFile::cached_files` names them for the page): a slow, deterministic build belongs in a cache by key, readable through `assets`
- `src/schema.json` lists every scene-file type and field for the editor; `src/schema.rs` tests it against the parser. When the parser gains a type or a field, or a default changes, update the schema (a missing type fails `cargo test schema`)
- `cargo run --release -- serve` hosts the builder at http://localhost:8787/web/ and renders layers for it (`src/server.rs`; `--port`, `--bind`, `--root`, `--allow-origin`)
- `.github/workflows/pages.yml` deploys the builder to GitHub Pages from master (`web/site.sh` assembles the site)

## Command-line Options
- `<FILE>`: Scene file (required, positional): our JSON, `.nff` (translated by `src/nff.rs`), or Tungsten's JSON (recognised by its `bsdfs` and `primitives`; translated by `src/tungsten.rs`). The image is written alongside it with a `.png` extension
- `--dump-scene <FILE>`: Write the scene as our JSON (for an NFF or Tungsten file, its translation)
- `-o, --output <FILE>`: Write the image here instead; `.png` (8-bit, display-encoded) or `.exr` (linear float)
- `-p, --progressive-render`: Update the output file during the render (at most once a second)
- `--width <NUM>`: Override scene file width
- `--height <NUM>`: Override scene file height
- `--seed <NUM>`: Override the scene's render seed; the same seed gives the same image
- `--crop <X> <Y> <W> <H>`: Render only the W by H pixels from column X and row Y (from the top left) of the whole `--width` by `--height` frame (`ImageOpts::crop`, `scene::Frame`): each pixel the frame's own, sample for sample (its camera ray and random streams are its place in the frame's, `ImageOpts::in_frame`; the camera and what is sized to its pixels are built for the frame), but where adaptive sampling's neighbours differ at the edges and image-wide glare and flare; path tracer only (the kernels number their pixels' streams within the image), and part of the saved render's key
- `--samples <NUM>`: Samples per pixel to render to, overriding the scene's `supersamples`
- `--add-samples <NUM>`: Render this many more samples per pixel than the saved state has
- `--fresh`: Start afresh rather than resuming
- `--noise-threshold <NUM>`: Adaptive sampling's threshold, overriding the scene's `adaptive` (0 turns it off)
- `--sample-map <FILE>`: Also write each pixel's samples, relative to the most any took
- `--time-map <FILE>`: Also write a profile: each pixel's render time (its thread's CPU time, summed over its samples; `src/time_map.rs`; under the wavefront's kernels a batch's time shared among its paths by the rays each traced) as an inferno heatmap scaled to the 99.5th percentile, or seconds in an `.exr`; not on the GPU, and only this run's samples (a resumed render needs `--fresh` for a whole map)
- Unasked, a scene renders with the first that can of: the GPU (a build with `--features gpu`, and a hardware adapter), the wavefront integrator (the kernels, on the CPU), the path tracer (saying why the others couldn't); the choice is printed (`- Integrator: ...`)
- `--wavefront`: Render with the wavefront integrator on the CPU, not the GPU (otherwise with the path tracer, saying why)
- `--gpu`: Render on the GPU, a software one too (lavapipe), if the scene can be flattened and the GPU path supports it (otherwise with the wavefront integrator or the path tracer, saying why)
- `--path-tracer`: Render with the recursive path tracer (`"integrator": "path"`)
- Renders are resumable by default: the accumulated samples are saved every 30 s, and at the end, to `<output>.accum` (exact sums and sums of squares, keyed by a hash of the scene file without its `supersamples`, `adaptive` and `output`, the size and seed overrides, and the integrator that rendered; so exposure, film and flare can be changed and the render refinished). Rerunning carries on where it stopped, or from a quick render adds samples up to the new target (`--samples 8`, later `--samples 64`); the result is identical to an uninterrupted render. Editing the scene starts afresh; after changing the renderer or files the scene refers to, use `--fresh`
- `--compare <FILE>`: Compare with a reference image (eg. `references/cornell-box/TungstenRender.exr`, fetched by `scripts/fetch-references.sh`): prints exposure fit, RMSE and mean FLIP error; writes `<output>.flip.png` and `<output>.reference.png`
- `--max-flip <NUM>`: With `--compare`, exit with status 2 if the mean FLIP error exceeds this
- `--depth <FILE>`: Also write a depth image (distance to the nearest surface through each pixel centre); `.exr` keeps distances, `.png` scales them for viewing

## Code Style
- Traits used for major abstractions (Camera, Geometry, Medium, MaterialModel)
- Box<dyn Trait> for polymorphism
- Error handling via Option<T>/Result<T,E> with unwrap()
- New code that turns a scene's RGB into light transport (a material's albedo, an emitter's colour) must convert it with `as_reflectance`, `as_illuminant` or `as_unbounded`, so spectral mode stays correct
- Full paths with crate:: prefix for imports
- 4-space indentation
- snake_case for variables and functions
- Structs using PascalCase
- Extensive use of Vector3<f64> from nalgebra
- Multithreaded rendering with Rayon

## Project Structure
- A library (`src/lib.rs`, the module tree) and the command-line tool (`src/main.rs`, behind the default `cli` feature: clap, indicatif, nv-flip and the other terminal and C dependencies). The library without `cli` builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` (check: `cargo check --lib --no-default-features --target wasm32-unknown-unknown`); keep C dependencies and getrandom out of it
- Modules organized by functionality (material, shapes, procedural)
- Scene definitions stored in JSON files (in demo/ and demo/scenes/); other renderers' formats are translated into that JSON (`SceneFile::read_document`; see `scene-formats.md`)
- Rendering progress tracking via indicatif
- Sample scenes available in demo/scenes/ with corresponding .png outputs (refreshed only when the user asks): scenes kept for their own sake (landscapes, skies, planets);
  features that only need showing to work go into `demo/scenes/stress-test.json` (plots of
  shapes, materials, lights, media, plants and camera effects in one garden; spectral, light
  BVH, motion blur, realistic lens, flare and adaptive sampling at once), not new scenes.
  `demo/scenes/christmas-tree.json` (written by `scripts/make-christmas-tree.py`) is a second
  stress test, of detail and lights at night: a decorated spruce (hundreds of bulb emitters on a
  curve wire, fur tinsel, baubles, presents) in a snow-laden forest, with fog; edit the script, not
  the JSON (`--preview` writes it cheaply lit, for framing).
  Fixed scenes for measurements (`scripts/variance.sh`) are in demo/benchmarks/. Forests' levels
  of detail are checked against full detail under many conditions (view, sun, species, density,
  season) by `scripts/lod-suite.py` (FLIP, brightness near and far, memory, time; results in
  `target/lod-suite/`, docs/research/learned-lod/suite.md)
- Render speed over history: `scripts/benchmark.py` builds master commits in a scratch worktree
  (target/bench) and renders `demo/benchmarks/teapot-sky.json` (mesh, plane, skysphere; kept in
  the oldest scene format so every commit can read it) 5 times each, appending time, rays and
  the image difference to the previous row to `demo/benchmarks/history.csv` (skipping commits
  already there; `--history N --every K` to backfill); `scripts/plot-benchmark.py` graphs it as SVG

## Development Best Practices
- Make small, incremental changes focused on a single concern
- Break complex features into smaller, sequential commits
- Commit frequently to create checkpoints (after each logical change)
- Run tests and linting before each commit
- Don't commit images (`.png`, `.gif`, `.mp4`, `.exr`: renders, comparisons, screenshots) unless the user asks for that image to be committed. Every version of an image stays in the history for good, and re-renders have made the repository hundreds of megabytes. Rendering a scene overwrites its tracked `.png` beside it: `git restore` it (or `-o` to a scratch path) rather than committing it, and check `git status` before committing. Show renders to the user as files from a scratch directory instead
- Create dedicated branches for each distinct feature
- Write descriptive commit messages explaining the purpose of changes
- Keep feature branches simple and focused on a single goal
- Verify each change works before moving to the next step
